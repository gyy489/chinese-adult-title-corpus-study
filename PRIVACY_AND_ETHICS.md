# Privacy and release boundary

The research object is title metadata, not audiovisual content, real events,
real identities, actual consent, or actual authorization. Public results must
not be interpreted as claims about people or events beyond the wording of the
titles.

The final analytical corpus contains 38,298 unique final de-identified title
texts. Before activation of that corpus, researcher-confirmed review led to 132
exact-span replacements across 100 titles; 11 reviewed catalogue-number titles
were retained and no entire title was excluded. Reapplying exact final-text
deduplication removed two additional record contributions. The post-build
direct-locator gate found zero matches in the six defined locator classes.

These controls reduce disclosure risk but do not establish complete anonymity
or independent anonymity certification. Accordingly, the public repository
contains only aggregate tables and research contracts. It excludes:

- raw or de-identified title strings;
- per-title hashes, record keys, provenance indices, and source identities;
- URLs, domains, contact identifiers, and evidence excerpts;
- model responses, private remediation evidence, and row-level human coding;
- submission correspondence, manuscript files, and internal research logs.

Two independent human coders reviewed the same 400-title sample only to assess
AI coding accuracy. Their coding is not treated as a gold standard and does not
replace the full-corpus AI labels.
